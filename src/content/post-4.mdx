## something acknowledged the measurement

The tape read exactly what I expected. Too exact. Suspiciously exact. I tapped it. It did not move. The pencil line went down perfectly straight. ***Too straight.*** I began to understand that I had made a mistake long before the saw touched the wood.

[do not proceed](https://dev.praxisproject.dev)

> proceed anyway

### the cut

You ever make a cut that *sounds* wrong? Not dull, not rough—just… *incorrect*. Like the saw is passing through something that isn’t entirely wood anymore.

- hesitate

Halfway through, the resistance vanished. Not decreased—vanished. The saw dropped slightly as if it had passed into empty space. I stopped. The board did not.

I pulled the saw away. The kerf was there. The board was separated. Everything appeared normal. But the pieces did not sit right. One half leaned toward the other, not falling, just… inclining.

Clamping helped. Not physically, but emotionally. The pieces were now forced into something resembling compliance. I could almost believe nothing was wrong.

almost

escalation: sanding ritual

I sanded the cut faces. Slowly. Carefully. The surface became smoother, but the feeling didn’t change. The pieces still didn’t belong together.

maybe if it's smoother it will make sense

It did not.

acceptance phase (partial)

I assembled the project anyway. You have to. There’s a point where stopping is worse than continuing. The final form exists now. It holds. It functions. It even looks correct—from certain angles, under certain lighting, if you don’t think about it too much.

final observation

Sometimes a cut is just a cut.

And sometimes—

it isn’t.
