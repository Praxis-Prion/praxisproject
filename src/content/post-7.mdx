It started with a simple thought: I should be able to see what’s going on. Not because anything was wrong, but because something could be wrong, and I wouldn’t know. This was unacceptable. So I decided to build a dashboard. Just one. Clean. Minimal. Useful.

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="first dashboard" class="float-middle" /> </a>
visibility increases questions

At first it was basic. CPU usage. Memory. Disk. The essentials. Then I thought, what about per-service metrics? That seemed important. Then, what about historical trends? Also important. Before long, I was tracking things I didn’t fully understand but felt strongly about observing.

## usage reality

After all this, I step back and admire the dashboard. It is comprehensive. Informative. Elegant.

Then I close it.

Days pass. Everything runs. I do not check the dashboard. Nothing alerts me, which is either good or suspicious.

brief return

At some point I open it again. The graphs are still moving. The numbers are still changing. It feels like walking into a room where something has been happening the whole time without you.

you missed nothing

or everything

## conclusion (observed)

The system is visible now. Completely observable. Every metric accounted for.

And yet, somehow, I still don’t know what’s going on.
