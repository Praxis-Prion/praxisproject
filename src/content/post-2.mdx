I picked up the board and it was straight. I am **certain** it was straight. I even held it up, sighted down the edge like a professional who knows what they’re doing, and thought `yes, this is acceptable wood`. The moment I marked it with a pencil, however, it developed a personality. A curve appeared. A twist manifested. The board has decided to explore new shapes and I am apparently just here to witness it.

## something is wrong (it is me)

`just take a little off` i whisper, like that has ever been true in the history of woodworking

[confidence continues to be misplaced](https://dev.praxisproject.dev)

The plane comes out. Smooth. Precise. Elegant. I take one pass—beautiful shaving, everything feels under control. I take another pass—slightly deeper, still fine. Third pass—**we have committed to a direction now**. There is no going back. The board is no longer straight in a *new and exciting way*. ***I have improved it into a worse version of itself.***

* one more pass
* just a touch more
* okay now it's thinner
* okay now it's thinner everywhere
* this is a different board now

```c#
int passes = 0;
while(boardIsWorse) {
  passes++;
  plane();
}
```

the more you fix it
the more it needs fixing
escalation protocol

At this point I introduce a square into the situation. The square does not agree with me. The square is judgmental. I rotate it. I flip it. I check from different angles like maybe geometry will change its mind. It does not. The square has never once doubted itself in its entire existence.

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="square betrayal" class="float-left" /> </a>

So I adjust. Carefully. Slowly. Thoughtfully. And by thoughtfully I mean I remove material in a way that creates an entirely new problem that did not exist before. Now I have two issues, which is more than I started with, which means I am making progress.

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="new problem unlocked" class="float-right" /> </a>
identify problem
fix problem
create additional problems
clamp phase (unavoidable)

I don’t need clamps yet, but I get them anyway. You can’t think without clamps nearby. They are emotional support tools. I tighten one just to see what happens. Nothing good happens, but something does happen, which feels like momentum.

how many clamps is enough?

incorrect question

The board is now being persuaded into shape. Not corrected—persuaded. There is a difference, and that difference is denial.

advanced reasoning

if i remove material from this side
and also from this side
and also from that side
eventually there will be no board and therefore no problem

perfection is the absence of material
```js
if(board == null) {
  problemSolved = true;
}
```
acceptance stage

I step back and observe what I have created. It is not straight. It is not square. But it is consistent in its refusal to be either, and that counts for something. There is a certain honesty to it now.

final conclusion (temporary)

The board was straight. I know it was. But somewhere along the way, between intention and execution, reality diverged. And now I have a piece that doesn’t match the plan—but does perfectly represent the process.
