It was supposed to be quick. DNS wasn’t resolving internally the way I wanted, and I thought, I’ll just fix that real fast. A small tweak. A tiny adjustment. Nothing major. I opened the config, made a change, restarted the service, and immediately lost resolution for everything.

the part where it used to work

The most unsettling part is that it was working. Not perfectly, but well enough. Now it’s not working at all, which feels like progress in the wrong direction.

## debugging begins

### verification loop

So I check things. Carefully. Methodically. Repeatedly.

check resolver config
check upstream
check local overrides
check /etc/hosts just in case
check the same things again but with more suspicion

why does one device resolve and another does not

#### cache is a concept

Now I remember: DNS caches exist. Everywhere. In the system, in the browser, in the router, possibly in the walls. I flush one cache. It changes nothing. I flush another. Something changes, but not in a way that helps.

cached result

cached mistake

At this point I am no longer sure what is real. I resolve a domain and get one IP. I resolve it again and get a different one. Both are technically correct, which is the worst outcome.

logs (cryptic)

The logs are active. They contain information. Possibly even useful information. Lines scroll by that look important, but none of them say “here is the problem.”

temporary fixes that feel permanent

I add a manual entry. It works. Of course it works. It bypasses everything I just spent time configuring. I tell myself it’s temporary. It is not temporary.

clarity (brief, fleeting)

Eventually, something clicks. One setting. One line. One small detail that explains everything. I fix it. Restart. Test again.

It works.

Everything works.

## conclusion (fragile)

The system is stable again. Resolution is correct. Services respond. The network feels coherent.

But I know the truth.

I will forget what fixed it.

And next time, I will say the same thing:

I’ll just fix DNS real quick
