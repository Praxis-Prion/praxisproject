It started with one port. Just one. I told myself, I only need access to this one service. Simple. Clean. Responsible. I forwarded it, refreshed the page, and it worked. That was the moment everything changed. Something unlocked in my brain that cannot be relocked.

the container spiral

Everything becomes a container. I no longer install things—I compose them. YAML files multiply. I open one and think who wrote this and then realize it was me, five minutes ago.

security awareness (sudden and intense)

At some point it hits me: this is exposed to the internet. Real internet. The one with bots. I watch the logs again. There are requests I did not make.

optimization that solves nothing

Now I care about performance. Not because anything is slow, but because it could be faster. I tweak configs. I restart services. I measure things that did not need measuring.

The system is now more complex and exactly as fast as before.

philosophical shift

Self-hosting is no longer about the service. The service is incidental. The real goal is control. Visibility. The ability to say I know exactly how this works even when that is objectively untrue.

final state (unstable)

Everything is running. Mostly. The services are accessible. The DNS resolves. The containers stay up. The logs are quiet—for now.

And yet, I know—

I will open another port.
