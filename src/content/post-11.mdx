I chose this plant specifically because it was labeled low maintenance. That phrase meant something to me. It suggested independence. Resilience. A mutual understanding that I would not need to be involved constantly. I planted it with confidence, thinking this one can handle itself.

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="initial optimism" class="float-middle" /> </a>
interpretation mismatch

Within days, it became clear that “low maintenance” is a relative term. Relative to what is unclear. The plant has needs. Many needs. Some of them change daily.

expectations vs reality

thrives on neglect

defines neglect very specifically

observation phase

Now I watch it. Not obsessively. Just… regularly. Frequently. Constantly.

leaves slightly drooping
leaves slightly perked up
leaves existing

this must mean something

intervention attempts

I adjust things. Small changes. Subtle tweaks.

more water
less water
more sun
less sun

Each change feels correct at the time. Each result feels inconclusive.

it responded

did it though

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="adjustments" class="float-left" /> </a>

The plant continues to exist in a way that suggests it is both fine and not fine simultaneously.

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="ongoing confusion" class="float-right" /> </a>
notice change
react to change
question reaction
external research (dangerous)

I look it up. Other people have grown this plant successfully. They describe it as easy. Effortless. Forgiving.

water occasionally
provide sunlight
ignore mostly

I am doing all of these things

trust issues

At some point I realize the problem may not be the plant. This realization is briefly acknowledged and then set aside.

the plant is fine

I am not fine

coexistence

We reach an understanding. I will continue to adjust things. The plant will continue to respond in ways I only partially understand. Together, we will maintain this delicate balance.

conclusion (tentative)

It is still alive. It is growing. It has not given up.

And neither have I.

But “low maintenance” remains—

a deeply subjective concept.
