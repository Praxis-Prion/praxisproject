I went into the shop with a plan. A *real* plan. Written down. Measurements, diagrams, even little arrows showing which way the grain should go like I’m some kind of organized human being. Five minutes later I’m staring at a board thinking `this seems about right` and suddenly we are freehanding reality. **The tape measure is now a suggestion. The pencil line is a rumor. The saw is a weapon.**

## this escalated immediately

`measure twice` they said
`cut once` they said
i measured once and emotionally committed

[confidence is key](https://dev.praxisproject.dev)

The first cut was wrong, obviously. Not *visibly* wrong at first. Oh no. That would be too easy. It was *subtly* wrong. The kind of wrong that waits until you’ve built three more parts before revealing itself like a villain monologue. ***Now everything must be adjusted to match the mistake.*** This is no longer woodworking. This is *error propagation engineering.*

* denial
* bargaining
* “i can fix this”
* making it worse
* clamps

```c#
bool thisIsFine = true;
while(thisIsFine) {
  cutAgain();
  thisIsFine = !measure();
}
```

the board knows what you did

the board remembers

anyway

So I grab clamps. Not because I need them yet, but because psychologically it feels like progress. One clamp becomes three. Three becomes seven. At some point I am no longer building anything—I am restraining the wood. The project is now a hostage situation.

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="too many clamps" class="float-left" /> </a>

Glue is involved now. This was not part of the plan. The glue has a timer. I can feel it judging me. Everything is sliding in directions that geometry does not support. I tighten one clamp and another part moves like it’s alive. The structure has opinions and I was not consulted.

<a href={testImage.src} target="_blank"> <Image src={testImage} alt="sliding joints" class="float-right" /> </a>
apply glue
panic
question life choices
sharpening will fix this (it will not)

At this point I decide the problem is my tools. Obviously. Not me. Never me. I spend twenty minutes sharpening a chisel to a mirror finish that could shave atoms. I return to the project with renewed confidence and immediately make a cleaner, sharper mistake.

is it sharper?

yes

did that help?

no

I step back. I look at what I’ve created. It is… something. Not what I intended, but undeniably a thing. The angles are suspicious. The joints are interpretive. The dimensions are more of a vibe.

philosophical interlude

what is square?
what is level?
what is a measurement if not a suggestion?

woodworking is just convincing trees to become furniture

advanced techniques
cutting slightly outside the line (on purpose, definitely)
sanding until the mistake becomes a feature
flipping the piece over and pretending that was always the front
saying “rustic” with confidence
```c#
if(projectLooksBad) {
  callItRustic();
}
```
final thoughts (there are no final thoughts)

In the end, I learned something. Not what I set out to learn, but something. The shelf leans slightly to the left, which I am choosing to interpret as character. The joints hold, mostly, which I am choosing to interpret as success. And the next time I go into the shop, I will absolutely, definitely, without question—

—do the exact same thing again.
